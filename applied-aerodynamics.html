PROJECT 01

Aerodynamic performance of the Bombardier CRJ900

A complete performance picture of a regional jet, built up from first principles: how much drag every part of the aircraft creates, how much thrust the engines can deliver as the aircraft climbs, and how tightly it can turn.

01.A

Component drag build-up

Rather than treating the aircraft as one shape, the zero-lift drag was computed component by component: fuselage, wings, empennage and landing gear. Each part's skin friction is scaled by a form factor for its shape and an interference factor for how it sits next to other parts, then everything is summed against the wing reference area.

$$C_{D_0} = \frac{\sum_{c} C_{f,c}\, FF_c\, Q_c\, S_{\text{wet},c}}{S_{\text{ref}}} + C_{D_{\text{misc}}}$$
  • \(C_{f,c}\): flat-plate skin-friction coefficient of the component, from its Reynolds number and Mach number.
  • \(FF_c\): form factor, which accounts for pressure drag due to thickness (fineness ratio for bodies, \(t/c\) and sweep for lifting surfaces).
  • \(Q_c\): interference factor between the component and its neighbours.
  • \(C_{D_{\text{misc}}}\): items that don't fit the build-up, such as the landing gear and upsweep.

01.B

Thrust analysis from sea level to 36,000 ft

PYTHON

A turbofan's thrust falls as the aircraft climbs because the air gets thinner. I wrote a Python model of the CRJ900's engines to produce thrust curves at altitudes from 0 to 36,000 ft, using the International Standard Atmosphere to get the air properties at each altitude.

The core of the model is the thrust lapse, which scales sea-level static thrust by the density ratio \(\sigma = \rho/\rho_0\):

$$\frac{T}{T_0} = \sigma^{\,m}, \qquad \sigma = \frac{\rho(h)}{\rho_0}$$

The exponent \(m\) depends on the engine type and its bypass ratio. In the troposphere, density follows the ISA lapse rate up to 36,089 ft, which is why 36,000 ft is a natural ceiling for the analysis.

01.C

Turn performance: the energy-manoeuvrability chart

EXCEL

An energy-manoeuvrability (E-M) chart shows on one plot how fast an aircraft can turn at every airspeed. I built the CRJ900's E-M chart in Excel. It is bounded by two limits: at low speed the wing runs out of lift (the stall boundary), and at high speed the structure runs out of strength (the limit load factor). Where the two meet is the corner speed, which gives the fastest possible turn.

$$\omega = \frac{g\sqrt{n^2-1}}{V}, \qquad R = \frac{V^2}{g\sqrt{n^2-1}}, \qquad n_{\text{stall}} = \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\rho V^2 S\, C_{L_{\max}}}{W}$$

PROJECT 02

Lift distribution on the Bushcat light aircraft

How does the choice of aerofoil and the shape of the wing change how lift is spread across the span? This project varied both on the Bushcat light-sport aircraft and compared Prandtl's lifting-line theory against higher-fidelity vortex lattice and panel methods.

02.A

Prandtl's lifting-line theory

MATLAB

Lifting-line theory models the wing as a single bound vortex along the span, shedding a sheet of trailing vortices. The circulation is written as a Fourier sine series, and the coefficients are found by enforcing the monoplane equation at stations across the span:

$$\alpha(\theta) - \alpha_{L=0}(\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} A_n \sin n\theta \left(\frac{4b}{a_0\, c(\theta)} + \frac{n}{\sin\theta}\right)$$

From the coefficients come the lift, the induced drag and the span efficiency factor \(e\), which measures how close the loading is to the ideal elliptical one:

$$C_L = \pi\, AR\, A_1, \qquad C_{D_i} = \pi\, AR \sum_{n} n A_n^2 = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi\, e\, AR}$$

The aerofoil enters through its lift-curve slope \(a_0\) and zero-lift angle \(\alpha_{L=0}\). The wing geometry enters through the chord distribution \(c(\theta)\) (taper and aspect ratio) and through twist in \(\alpha(\theta)\).

The "peak \(c_l/C_L\)" readout shows where along the span the section lift coefficient is highest, which is roughly where the wing will stall first. A rectangular wing peaks at the root, which is benign. Strong taper pushes the peak towards the tip, which is why tapered wings usually need washout.

02.B

Comparing against higher-fidelity methods

Lifting-line theory is fast but assumes a straight, high-aspect-ratio wing. To check how far it can be trusted on the Bushcat's geometry, the same configurations were modelled with methods that make fewer assumptions:

FIDELITY · LOW Lifting line Prandtl's single bound vortex, solved in MATLAB. Captures the spanwise loading from aspect ratio, taper and twist.
FIDELITY · MEDIUM Tornado VLM A vortex lattice of horseshoe vortices across span and chord, so chordwise loading, sweep and low aspect ratio are handled.
FIDELITY · HIGHER XFLR5 Viscous aerofoil polars from XFoil combined with 3D panel and VLM analysis, so the real aerofoil shape and viscous effects are included.
