The Gantt chart, made to be pulled about

02 · THE METHOD

How the critical path is found

The critical path method needs only two sweeps over the network. A forward pass gives each task its earliest start and finish; a backward pass from the project end gives the latest times that still hold the finish date. The difference is the float.

$$ES_j=\max_{i\in\text{pred}(j)}EF_i,\qquad EF_j=ES_j+d_j,\qquad LF_i=\min_{j\in\text{succ}(i)}LS_j,\qquad LS_i=LF_i-d_i$$
$$\text{float}_i = LS_i - ES_i,\qquad \text{critical path}=\{\,i:\ \text{float}_i = 0\,\}$$

For this programme the forward pass runs A (10 d), C (12), D (20), E (8), F (25), G (18), I (30), N (16), O (12), R (20), X (10), Y (5): 10 + 12 + 20 + 8 + 25 + 18 + 30 + 16 + 12 + 20 + 10 + 5 = 186 days. Steel procurement (B) looks urgent but has 25 days of float because the foundations take longer than the steel does to arrive.

04 · MONEY AND PEOPLE

The banana curve and the crew histogram

Cost accumulates as work is done. If every task starts as early as it can, money is spent sooner (amber); if every task starts as late as it can, later (blue). Any real schedule lives in the band between, which is why it is called the banana curve. The crew chart stacks the people on site by department and shows the peak: a programme that needs 58 people on site at its peak and about ten in its first weeks will not be resourced as drawn, which is when resource levelling starts moving tasks within their float.

05 · RISK

Plan for being wrong: a Monte Carlo run

A single finish date is a lie of precision. Give every task an optimistic and a pessimistic duration (here between about 0.8× and 1.8× the plan, skewed late because work overruns more often than it underruns), draw each duration at random from a triangular distribution, recompute the critical path, and repeat three thousand times. The histogram is the answer to "when will it really finish?".

Finish-date distribution · 3 000 runs
white = baseline plan
Pick a contingency and see how likely the plan becomes.

The lesson is the gap between the plan and the median. Even when every task is on its most likely duration, the project finishes on its baseline date only rarely, because several near-critical paths all have to go right at once (the effect called merge bias). The right-hand chart is the criticality index: how often each task sat on the critical path across all runs. It is how you find the tasks that deserve a manager's attention, which are not always the ones that are critical on paper.

06 · THE LIFECYCLE

Where this sits in a project's life

01 INITIATE

Scope and charter

What is being built, for whom, and what counts as done. The work breakdown structure turns scope into the 25 tasks above.

02 PLAN

Schedule, cost, resource

Durations, logic links, calendars and crews go into the model; the critical path, S-curve and histogram fall out of it.

03 EXECUTE

Coordinate departments

Six trades hand work to each other. A baseline is saved so every later change is visible, as the grey ticks are here.

04 CONTROL

Track, crash, re-plan

When a task slips, crashing buys days back from the cheapest critical task; the Monte Carlo run says how much contingency is honest.

05 CLOSE

Snag, hand over, learn

Snagging and handover (X, Y) sit on the critical path: the end of the project is often the most expensive place to lose a day.

The programme on this page is a representative construction schedule written for this demonstration, with illustrative durations, crews and costs. The method is the one used in MS Project: logic links, a critical path, a baseline, resource histograms and cash-flow curves; what is different here is that all of it can be touched.
