:::worked Is the secondary text readable?
The pages of this website use the page background #070a1a and three text colours: `--text-muted` #9aa0c3, `--accent` #a78bfa and `--text-faint` #5f668f. Compute the contrast ratio of each against the background and say which meet AA for normal text.

**Step 1. Luminance of the background.** #070a1a has R = 7, G = 10, B = 26. Dividing by 255 gives 0.0275, 0.0392 and 0.1020. The first two are at most 0.03928, so they are divided by 12.92 to give 0.00213 and 0.00303; the third is converted by ((0.1020 + 0.055) / 1.055)^{2.4} = 0.01033. Then L = 0.2126 × 0.00213 + 0.7152 × 0.00303 + 0.0722 × 0.01033 = 0.00045 + 0.00217 + 0.00075 = 0.00337.

**Step 2. Luminance of the text colours** (computed the same way): #9aa0c3 gives 0.3595, #a78bfa gives 0.3358 and #5f668f gives 0.1392.

**Step 3. Ratios.** For #9aa0c3: (0.3595 + 0.05) / (0.00337 + 0.05) = 0.4095 / 0.05337 = 7.67. For #a78bfa: 0.3858 / 0.05337 = 7.23. For #5f668f: 0.1892 / 0.05337 = 3.54.

**Step 4. Verdict.** The muted and accent colours pass AA comfortably (7.67 and 7.23 against 4.5). The faint colour does not for normal text: 3.54 is below 4.5, although it passes the 3 : 1 limit for large text and for decorative outlines.

**What to do.** Keep the faint colour for non-essential detail such as axis ticks and decoration, never for text a visitor must read, or lighten it until it clears 4.5. This is a real finding about this website, and it is the sort of thing only measurement reveals: to the eye of a designer looking at a good monitor in a dim room, #5f668f looks perfectly acceptable.
:::
