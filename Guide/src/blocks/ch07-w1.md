:::worked Where does a point land on the screen?
A camera has a vertical field of view of 36 degrees, and the canvas is 1000 by 562 pixels. A point lies in front of the camera at camera-space coordinates (0.1, 0.05, −0.5) metres. Find its pixel position.

**Step 1. The focal factor.** θ/2 = 18°, tan 18° = 0.32492, so f = 1 / 0.32492 = 3.0777.

**Step 2. Aspect ratio.** a = 1000 / 562 = 1.7794.

**Step 3. Normalised coordinates.** The distance −z is 0.5. Then x_ndc = (3.0777 / 1.7794) × (0.1 / 0.5) = 1.7296 × 0.2 = 0.3459, and y_ndc = 3.0777 × (0.05 / 0.5) = 0.3078.

**Step 4. Pixels.** x_px = (0.3459 × 0.5 + 0.5) × 1000 = 0.6730 × 1000 = 673.0. y_px = (−0.3078 × 0.5 + 0.5) × 562 = 0.3461 × 562 = 194.5.

**Step 5. Sanity check.** The point is to the right of and above the centre of the view, so the pixel should be right of 500 and above (smaller than) 281. It is: (673, 194.5). If the point were twice as far away, at z = −1.0, its normalised offsets would halve and it would land at (586.5, 237.8), nearer the centre.
:::
