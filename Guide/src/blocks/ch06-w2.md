:::worked A frame-rate independent camera
The camera of the robot page eases towards its target with snappiness s = 5.5 per second. Find the fraction of the gap closed per frame at 30, 60 and 144 hertz, and the fraction closed in half a second.

**Step 1. Per-frame fraction.** k = 1 − exp(−5.5 × Δt), with Δt = 1/30, 1/60 and 1/144.

**Step 2. Evaluate.** At 30 Hz: 5.5/30 = 0.1833, exp(−0.1833) = 0.8325, so k = 0.1675. At 60 Hz: 5.5/60 = 0.0917, exp(−0.0917) = 0.9124, so k = 0.0876. At 144 Hz: 5.5/144 = 0.0382, exp(−0.0382) = 0.9625, so k = 0.0375.

**Step 3. Check that they agree.** At 30 Hz, half a second is 15 frames, and the gap remaining is (1 − 0.1675)^15 = 0.8325^15 = 0.0639. At 60 Hz it is 30 frames of 0.9124: 0.9124^30 = 0.0639. At 144 Hz it is 72 frames of 0.9625: also 0.0639. The remaining gap is the same on all three screens: 6.4%.

**Step 4. Closed form.** The fraction closed in half a second is 1 − e^{−5.5 × 0.5} = 1 − e^{−2.75} = 1 − 0.0639 = 0.936, matching the product of per-frame factors, as it must. After half a second the camera is 93.6% of the way there.
