The engine behind the CRJ-700

The CF34-8 is the newest member of the CF34 family. It shares its core with the CF34-3 on the CRJ-200 but adds a larger fan, a 10-stage high-pressure compressor and digital control, which lets the 90-seat regional jet fly further on less fuel.

Debrief

How close did the model get?

I checked the hand calculation against published CF34-8 data and an independent Jenkinson thrust estimate. It nails thrust and pressure ratio, and it misses on bypass ratio.

There is no published cruise thrust to check against. The hand calculation matched published takeoff thrust to 3.3 %, and unlike the Jenkinson curves it accounts for mass flow, pressure ratios and the thermodynamic state at every station, so I treated it as the better cruise estimate. The two differ by about 47 %.

Most likely from the assumed mass flow of 240 kg/s used in the cruise iteration, and from assuming constant air density and fan performance. Those assumptions leave out the losses and flow restrictions in a real bypass duct.

I started from 95 % and 90 %, then iterated. 80 % for the compressors and 85 % for the turbines gave the best agreement with published data, and I applied them at both sea level and cruise.

Replace the lumped single-stream cycle with a real bypass stream, use component maps rather than fixed efficiencies, and let the gas properties vary through the combustor so mass flow and bypass ratio come out of the model instead of being assumed.
