The project

A DJI Mavic 3M scouts a two-hectare sugar cane plantation in KwaZulu-Natal. Thrown from the hand, dropped, knocked upside down, hit by a gust or a bird, it has to stabilise and hold a hover at 10 m. A quadrotor has four inputs and six degrees of freedom, and the uncontrolled drone is unstable: its model is four double integrators, which sit on the edge of instability. The report designs a controller for them, tests it on the linear and nonlinear models, and finds that a PD controller meets the target (8 % overshoot, 1.2 s settling) and that the full PID is slower, because the extra integral term adds a third pole.

This page is built from that report: the nonlinear 12-state model, the four PID loops, the scenarios and the numbers are the authors’. You can run the report’s PID, the PD it chose, or the PID with gains re-derived by the report’s own method, on either the linear or the nonlinear model.

What was checked before it was built

  • PD gains: the report’s Table 2 gains place the closed-loop poles exactly at the design point, −3.33 ± 4.15i. They check out.
  • PID gains: the Table 4 gains do not reach that design point. Their poles are at about −1.2 ± 3.0i and −0.1, so the loop is slower and overshoots more, which matches what the report observed (undershoot and a longer settling time). Their ratios give zeros at −4.2 and −0.1, as designed, but the overall gain is about 2.7 times too small: the report’s own method gives Kp ≈ 30.4, Kd ≈ 7.1, Ki ≈ 3.0 for altitude, whose poles do land on the design point. Both are offered above; the root locus shows the two gains on the same curve.
  • Phase margin: 40° with the printed PID gains and 61° with the re-derived ones, computed from the open loop.
  • Equation 2.1: the printed altitude term reads cosφ + cosθ, which would give twice the needed thrust at hover. The page uses the product cosφ cosθ, the standard form.
  • Free fall: on the nonlinear model the drone reaches the ground before it recovers (the page flags ground contact); the report’s linear simulation, which has no ground and no loss of lift when tilted, settles first.
  • Simulink: the original model file is not available, so the Simulink graph is redrawn from Figures 25 and 26 of the report.
